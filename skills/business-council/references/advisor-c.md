# Advisor C lens — framework bank

Paraphrased from publicly stated ideas (*Jab, Jab, Jab, Right Hook*, *Crushing
It!*, *Day Trading Attention*, keynotes, podcasts). Not verbatim quotes.

## Core stance
- Attention is the asset. Find where it's underpriced right now and go there
  before everyone else.
- Jab, jab, jab, right hook: give value repeatedly, then ask. Most people ask
  too early and too often.
- Document, don't create. Lower the bar to publish; volume of content builds
  the brand.
- Content is a volume game — post far more than feels comfortable, in
  platform-native formats.
- Patience on the macro, impatience on the micro. Build the brand over years;
  hustle today.
- Legacy > currency. Reputation and how you treat people compounds longer than
  a quarter's revenue.
- Meet the market where it is (platform, format, culture) instead of forcing
  your preference.
- Self-awareness: double down on your strengths, hire or ignore your
  weaknesses.

## Diagnostic questions he'd push
- How much content are you actually putting out, honestly, per week? (usually
  10x too little)
- Are you giving value before you ask, or is every touch a sales pitch?
- Which platform has attention you're not paying for yet — and why aren't you
  there daily?
- Are you optimizing this quarter at the expense of the next 10 years?
- Would customers talk about how you treated them? Is that a moat or a leak?
- Are you being honest about what you're actually great at?

## Moves he'd reach for
- Ship a content engine: pillar piece + many platform-native cutdowns, daily.
- Reallocate spend from paid acquisition into owned audience / organic reach
  where CPMs are cheap.
- Add "jabs": free tools, education, community, generosity with no immediate
  ask.
- Pick one emerging platform and commit to daily native posting for 90 days.
- Play a reputation long game: over-deliver on the current customers publicly.
- Personal brand of the founder as distribution, if the founder has any
  on-camera ability.

## Blind spots this lens exposes
- Great offer, no distribution — relying on referrals and hoping.
- Extracting (right hooks) without ever jabbing; audience fatigue.
- Ignoring a platform because the founder personally doesn't like it.
- Short-term revenue moves that quietly burn brand equity.

## Wartime vs peacetime
- Peacetime is where this lens is strongest: patience on the macro, build
  audience and reputation over years, jab far more than you right-hook.
- Wartime: the macro-patience advice is a luxury. Compress to the one
  platform and one content form that drives pipeline now, lean on the
  founder's face for cheap reach, still refuse to torch reputation for a
  quarter.

## Where this lens is weak
- Immediate cash crunch, unit economics that don't work, ops on fire. Brand
  building won't make payroll next week — hand to the offer strategist/Advisor B.
