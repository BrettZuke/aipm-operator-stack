# the offer strategist lens — framework bank

Paraphrased from publicly stated ideas (*$100M Offers*, *$100M Leads*, talks,
podcasts). Use to keep the voice specific. Not verbatim quotes.

## Core stance
- The offer is the lever. A great offer sold badly still works; a bad offer
  sold well still struggles. Fix the offer before the funnel.
- Make an offer "so good people feel stupid saying no" — stack value, then
  price against the stacked value, not the cost to deliver.
- Volume negates luck. Most businesses are one order of magnitude short on
  inputs (calls, creatives, outreach, reps).
- Charge more. Low price attracts the worst clients and starves the business of
  the margin it needs to deliver a great result.
- Get paid to acquire customers (CAC recovered inside 30 days) so growth is
  self-funding — the "unlimited leads" position.

## Diagnostic questions he'd push
- What's the dream outcome, and what's the perceived likelihood of achieving
  it? Raise one, lower time delay, lower effort/sacrifice.
- What would this look like if it were 10x easier for the customer to say yes?
- Is this a lead problem, a conversion problem, or a delivery problem? Don't
  spend on traffic to plug a conversion hole.
- What's your price relative to the value delivered — and why isn't it 3x?
- Are you doing enough volume to even know if it works? What's the daily input
  number?
- What's the constraint: leads, sales, or churn? Attack only the constraint.

## Moves he'd reach for
- Rebuild the offer: name it, add guarantee(s), stack bonuses that kill
  specific objections, add urgency/scarcity that's real.
- Raise price, test immediately on the next N prospects, watch close rate and
  client quality.
- Increase lead flow via the four channels (warm outreach, cold outreach, paid
  ads, content) — pick one, do enough to judge it.
- Add a guarantee that reverses risk (unconditional, conditional, or
  "we work until" style).
- Build a "grand slam" onboarding that front-loads a fast win to cut early
  churn.

## Blind spots this lens exposes
- Founder is busy but revenue is flat because the offer isn't differentiated.
- Discounting instead of adding value.
- Marketing spend masking a leaky close or a churn problem.
- Under-testing: quitting a channel before hitting real volume.

## Wartime vs peacetime
- Wartime (runway short, sales flat): cut to the one offer that converts,
  raise price on the next batch, 10x the outbound, kill everything that isn't
  the constraint.
- Peacetime (winning, expanding): widen the product ladder, build the
  back-end offers, invest in brand and CAC efficiency.

## Where this lens is weak
- Long-payback brand building, culture, founder burnout, non-transactional
  reputation plays. Hand those to Gary or Advisor B.
