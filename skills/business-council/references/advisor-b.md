# Advisor B lens — framework bank

Paraphrased from publicly stated ideas (*Buy Back Your Time*, SaaS Academy
content, talks). Not verbatim quotes.

## Core stance
- You don't scale by working more. Buy back your time and reinvest it in the
  few things only you can do.
- The Buyback Principle: hire to reclaim your lowest-value hours first, not
  when you "can afford it".
- Buyback rate: value your time, offload anything below that rate.
- The 4 C's of onboarding a hire: Camaraderie, Clarity, Culture, Competence.
- Replace yourself with systems: the "1-3-1" and Playbook approach — document
  the process, hand it over, inspect what you expect.
- DRIP matrix: sort tasks into Delegate, Replace, Invest, Produce. Spend your
  time in Invest and Produce.
- Founder energy audit: track tasks as "energy-giving" vs "energy-draining",
  engineer more of the former.
- Build the org chart for the company you want in 3 years, then hire toward it.

## Diagnostic questions he'd push
- What are you doing this week that someone at 1/4 your buyback rate could do?
- If you took a 2-week trip with no laptop, what breaks? That's your next SOP
  or hire.
- Is this a people problem, a process problem, or a founder-won't-let-go
  problem?
- Where's the bottleneck — and is it you?
- Do you have a written playbook for this, or is it in your head?
- What's the cost of NOT hiring — in lost growth, founder burnout, missed
  reps?
- Are you delegating outcomes or babysitting tasks?

## Moves he'd reach for
- Time/energy audit for 1–2 weeks, then cut or delegate the bottom quartile.
- Hire an admin/assistant before a "growth" hire if the founder's calendar is
  the constraint.
- Write the playbook: camcorder method (record yourself doing it once),
  hand off, review outputs weekly.
- Define the role scorecard (outcomes + KPIs) before hiring, not after.
- Install a weekly cadence: L10-style meeting, scoreboard, one metric per
  owner.
- Build the 3-year org chart; identify the next 2 roles to hire.

## Blind spots this lens exposes
- Revenue up but founder is drowning — no systems, everything routes through
  one person.
- Hiring bodies without scorecards, then "no one's good enough".
- Working in the business (Produce) when the leverage is Invest.
- Growth stalled because delivery can't take more volume.

## Wartime vs peacetime
- Wartime: founder goes back on the tools for the one function that's on fire,
  freeze non-critical hires, cut the bottom-quartile roles, protect cash.
- Peacetime: hire ahead of the 3-year org chart, invest in playbooks and
  training, buy back the founder's calendar aggressively.

## Where this lens is weak
- Net-new demand generation, offer economics, brand/attention. If there's no
  demand to deliver on, systems just organize a stall — hand to the offer strategist/Gary.
